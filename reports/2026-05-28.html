⭐ 211 stars today

Fully automatic censorship removal for language models

Heretic is a fully automatic censorship-removal tool for open-weight language models. It applies "abliteration" — a technique that identifies and surgically zeros out the direction in a transformer's activation space responsible for refusals — and does so end-to-end without requiring users to hand-pick layers, prompts, or refusal datasets.

Written in Python and built on top of the standard Hugging Face transformer stack, Heretic automates the full pipeline: probing the model with calibration prompts, isolating the refusal direction, computing the orthogonal projection, and writing out a modified checkpoint. The result is a model that retains its general capabilities while no longer reflexively refusing benign queries that tripped its safety tuning.

It is trending because abliteration has gone from a research curiosity to a community sport around open-weight releases (Llama, Qwen, Mistral, etc.), and a one-command tool that produces an uncensored variant is exactly what the local-LLM crowd has been asking for. Note that the project sits in ethically and legally contested territory, and its README pitches it primarily for research and personal use.
